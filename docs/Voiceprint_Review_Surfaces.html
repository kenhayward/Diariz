Diariz · speaker identification

Two Queues, One Voiceprint

Review Voice Matches and the Voiceprint tab look like two views of the same list. They are not. They are two stages of one lifecycle, and they never hold the same speaker at the same time.

Where speakers come from

Every speaker gets exactly one verdict when its recording is transcribed. That verdict decides which surface it appears on — and a speaker can only ever be on one of them.

What each surface is for

The permission split is the design, not an accident. Whoever was in the meeting can say whether a voice is a given person. Only a directory manager should be reshaping other people’s biometrics.

Why “confirm” feels ambiguous

Three separate assertions live in this system, and two of them are called some form of confirm. They are kept apart on purpose — a recording can genuinely be the right person and still be too noisy to learn from — but the naming does not say so.

“Review queue” also names two different lists — the suggestions inbox, and the Voiceprint tab’s worth checking set (the impostor, alone and unlinked verdicts). Same phrase, different populations, different questions.

The one real overlap

Both surfaces can change which audio trains a voiceprint, and they do it through the same field and the same mechanism: write SpansJson, clear UsedMs, stamp RecomputeQueuedAt, queue a re-embed. That is deliberate — one way of saying it, not two.

They differ in one way that matters. In the inbox the segment marks are one-way and provisional: remove only, and nothing is written unless you confirm. On the Voiceprint tab they are two-way and stored, seeded from what is already saved. So the inbox can only narrow a first enrolment; the tab is the only place to widen one back out, or reset to the whole speaker.

Two defects found in review

Open · issue 662

Confirming a voice does not record that a human listened

Answering in the inbox is premised on having listened — that is why it plays audio, and why voices with no audio are withheld. But accepting writes no ConfirmedAt. The recording lands on the Voiceprint tab unconfirmed and immediately joins that tab’s worth checking list, asking a directory manager to listen to audio that was just listened to.

Fixed · 0.259.7

Nothing said that removing a segment shapes the voiceprint

Taking a segment out decides what the voiceprint learns from. The control said “Take this segment out” — out of what unstated; the count said “1 segment excluded” — from what unstated; and the introduction never mentioned segments at all. Every control that removes one now names the voiceprint, and the panel says that nothing is saved until you answer.

How to use them

Routine · everyone

Work the inbox

Open Review Voice Matches, listen, take out any segments that are somebody else, then confirm or decline. Your own recordings, no permission needed. Nothing else needs doing here.

Occasional · directory managers

Repair a drifting voiceprint

When Diariz starts naming the wrong person, go to the Voiceprint tab. It is the only place that shows the impostor and alone verdicts, spans other people’s recordings, and can reassign a misattributed speaker or drop bad audio from training.

The short version: the inbox is where identities get decided; the tab is where a voiceprint gets repaired.
